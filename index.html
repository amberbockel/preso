UX 10104 · The myth
User experience design

So what is UX?

EVERY DIGITAL THING NEEDS A UX DESIGNER
PHONES
TABLETS
LAPTOPS
WATCHES
CAMERAS
VOICE

UX stands for user experience.

A UX designer's job is to make sure that when you pick up an app, it just makes sense — you know where to tap, you find what you need, and you don't get lost or annoyed.

They design the path, not just the picture.

Quick definition

"How it feels to use this thing."

— every UX designer, basically

UX 10105 · Definition
A big part of UX today

AI isn't a robot
that knows everything.

THINK OF IT LIKE…

A really fast,
really powerful intern.

Great at following patterns. Doesn't actually know anything until somebody teaches it — that somebody is a designer.

Designing for AI = three jobs
  • ·Guardrails — AI says weird or wrong things. Designers build the safety nets.
  • ·Personality — serious professor, or chill friend? That's a design choice.
  • ·Intent — AI has to guess what you actually want. Designers help it read the room.

At Google we call this Agentic UX — the tech does the work for you, so you spend more time being creative and less time clicking buttons.

UX 10106 · AI, really
You already know the difference

Bad UX

A door that says PUSH but you have to pull.

Doors with flat handles. Apps that hide the back button. Forms that make you start over because of one typo.

Makes you feel dumb. (You're not.)

Good UX

A door with a handle that says "pull me."

Snapchat opens straight to the camera. The volume button does what you expect. Undo exists.

You don't even notice it. That's the goal.

UX 10116 · Quantity
Discussion · 10 minutes
Step 1 · Your favorite apps aren't magic

What's one app you use every single day?

Shout it out.

A game, a social app, a tool for school. Don't filter.

Discord Roblox Spotify YouTube TikTok Snapchat
Then I dig in.
  • ·"What problem does this solve for you?"
  • ·If they say boredom → "Connect with friends, or find new music?"
  • ·If they say Discord → "Why is this better than texting?"

Every app you love exists because it solves a problem better than the thing before it.

UX 10117 · Ask & recall
Solo · 5 minutes · paper out
Step 2 · Find an energy leak

Write down one thing that frustrates you daily.

It can be inside an app you already use — or out in the real world.

  • ·Finding your gym shoes in the morning.
  • ·Keeping track of homework across five teachers.
  • ·Group chats that won't stop pinging.
  • ·Splitting the bill at lunch.

Write your problem at the top of the paper. One sentence. That's it.

Amber's pro tip

Designers look for energy leaks — the moments you feel annoyed, stuck, or like you're doing the same dumb thing for the tenth time.

That's exactly what you should write down.

UX 10121 · Sketch
Pairs · 5 minutes · 2 each way
Step 4 · Show your messy sketches

Turn to your neighbor.

You show.

All 8 boxes. Don't apologize for them. Don't explain. Just show.

2 minutes. Then swap.

They tell you two things.
  • ✓"The most helpful part is…"
  • ?"One thing that's still confusing is…"

Not "this is cool." Not "this won't work." Specific + curious.

In tech we never build the first thing we think of. We show messy sketches to teammates so the idea gets stronger.

UX 10122 · Crit
Solo · 2 minutes · flip the paper
Step 5 · Take the feedback. Pick one. Refine.

Final frame.

Take the feedback you just got. Pick your one best idea. Flip the paper over and draw a more flushed-out version — the whole back of the page.

Make it clear enough that I could understand it without you saying a word.

"Flushed out" doesn't mean perfect art. It means clarity. A strong composition. A clear button. That's all.

UX 10123 · Refine
Solo · refine your favorite

5:00

Make it real.

Take the feedback. Pick the one idea you love most. Draw it bigger, with labels — like it's a real screen.

UX 10124 · Refine
Volunteers · 5 minutes · 3–5 sketches
Step 6 · Show the room

Who wants to share theirs?

When you stand up, two things only.
  • ·The problem you picked. One sentence.
  • ·The solution. Hold up the page. Point at it.

No apologizing. No "it's not done." It's never done. Show it anyway.

What we just did.

Identified a problem → brainstormed → got feedback → iterated → shared.

That's the exact process we use at Google. You just did it.
